## 1. Executive Summary: How to Win Harshita Arora in 10 Minutes

If [Harshita Arora](https://www.ycombinator.com/people/harshita-arora) is on your Y Combinator interview panel, you are pitching the **youngest General Partner in YC history**—a self-taught builder from Saharanpur, Uttar Pradesh who dropped out of school after 8th grade at age 15, shipped a top-2 US App Store finance app from her bedroom at 16, hacked the US O-1 "Extraordinary Ability" visa system as a teenager, and pivoted [AtoB](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/atob) during COVID-19 by spending weeks interviewing truck drivers at California truck stops ([YC Blog](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/welcome-harshita), [TEDxMAIT Talk](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SaRzFRw30v4), [Forbes Interview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=M25bfmXt82s)).

**What makes Harshita say "Yes" in the interview room:**

1. **Uncredentialed, rule-hacking high agency ("What you shipped beats your resume"):** Having bypassed high school, IIT, and Stanford herself, she has zero reverence for pedigree for its own sake. She gravitates toward teenage prodigies, dropouts, repeat builders, and resourceful outsiders who bend bureaucracy to ship ([Startup Samadhan](https://startupsamadhan.com/harshita-arora-youngest-y-combinator-general-partner-atob-fintech-story/), [Soundarya Balasubramani O-1 Breakdown](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6Urp5SrKYto)).
2. **Physical, ground-level customer discovery ("Going to the truck stop"):** When COVID killed AtoB's original commuter-bus idea during YC S20, Harshita and her co-founders drove to truck stops in Stockton, California for weeks with Amazon gift cards to interview truckers about 3:00 AM fuel-card outages ([Forbes Interview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=M25bfmXt82s), [Founders You Should Know Pitch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=21pkv8s2vms)). She looks for founders who physically go to where the pain lives—or run *hundreds* of discovery calls (like her portfolio company [Laurence](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/laurence))—before locking in their wedge.
3. **Modernizing "boring" legacy plumbing & low-NPS monopolies:** She loves unglamorous, trillion-dollar industries running on 1980s/1990s rails (trucking payments, freight brokerage, mortgage origination, government contracting) where incumbents like WEX and Fleetcor have *"lower Net Promoter Scores than Comcast and airlines"* ([Founders You Should Know Pitch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=21pkv8s2vms)).
4. **Tiny 2–3 person technical teams selling AI directly to the Fortune 100:** In her [Summer 2026 Request for Startups ("Startups That Want to Sell to Huge Companies")](https://ycinsight.com/ideas/selling-to-biggest-companies), co-authored with Brad Flora, she rejects the old dogma that startups can only sell to other startups first. She backs tiny technical teams solving one quantifiable pain point for one specific team inside a giant enterprise to land $200K–$2M contracts within months.
5. **Frontier AI data, RL environments, and agent harnesses:** Across W26, S26, F26, and W27, she has been one of YC's most active Primary Partners for **post-training RL environments, rights-cleared multimodal/computer-use data labs, and agent evaluation infrastructure** ([Polymath](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/polymath), [Luel](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/luel), [Datoric](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/datoric), [Belvedir](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/belvedir), [Ashr](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/ashr), [Markov](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/markov)).

## 4. Harshita's Requests for Startups (RFS) & Active Investment Theses

### 1. Summer 2026 RFS: "Startups That Want to Sell to Huge Companies" (Co-authored with Brad Flora)
* **The Core Thesis ([YC Insight](https://ycinsight.com/ideas/selling-to-biggest-companies), [FundReef](https://fundreef.substack.com/p/ycs-summer-2026-wishlist-15-startups)):** The old startup rulebook—*"sell only to other startups first, grow slowly, and wait years before moving upmarket to enterprise"*—is dead in the AI era. Fortune 100 companies collectively spend $50B+/year on tech, have board-mandated AI budgets growing 40% YoY, and have created fast-track "innovation" procurement lanes that compress buying cycles from 18 months down to **3–6 months**.
* **How a 2–3 Person Startup Wins:** A Fortune 10 company may have 200 internal AI use cases identified, but their internal IT team only has capacity to build 10. Harshita and Brad emphasize that **the Fortune 100 is not one monolithic customer—it is hundreds of individual teams, each with its own budget and pain points**. If a 2–3 person technical team solves *one* specific problem exceptionally well for *one* team (e.g., saving 2,000 hours/year or reducing error rates by 50%), they can land **$200K–$2M initial contracts** during or right after the YC batch and expand to $5M–$20M within two years ([YC Insight](https://ycinsight.com/ideas/selling-to-biggest-companies)).

### 2. AI Infrastructure: Post-Training RL Environments, Expert Data & Agent Evals
Looking across her Primary Partner investments ([Polymath](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/polymath), [Luel](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/luel), [Datoric](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/datoric), [Belvedir](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/belvedir), [Ashr](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/ashr), [Markov](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/markov), [Codag](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/codag)), Harshita has a clear thesis on where the AI stack bottleneck sits in 2026–2027:
* **Rights-Cleared Multimodal & Computer-Use Data:** Public web data is exhausted and synthetic pipelines degrade. She backs fast, audit-ready data collection engines capturing real human actions, voice, robotics, and professional CAD/software screen traces ([Luel](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/luel), [Datoric](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/datoric), [Markov](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/markov)).
* **Long-Horizon RL Environments & Private Post-Training:** Moving beyond single-turn prompts to simulated multi-day worlds where agents learn judgment ([Polymath](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/polymath)), autonomous LoRA post-training on company logs ([Belvedir](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/belvedir)), and overnight agent evaluation swarms ([Ashr](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/ashr)).

### 3. AI-Native Disruptors in Freight, Fintech & Legacy Operations
Drawing directly from her [AtoB](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/atob) playbook, she actively backs founders replacing phone-and-paper middlemen in massive real-world transactions—such as [LinkLane](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/linklane) (AI freight brokerage in the $1T trucking market), [Copperlane](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/copperlane) (agentic mortgage origination), and [Capto](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/capto) (AI federal contract capture and proposal writing).
