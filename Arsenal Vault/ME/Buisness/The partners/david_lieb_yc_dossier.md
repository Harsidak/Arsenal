### Masterclass: *"How To Keep Your Users"* by David Lieb

* **Source:** [YouTube Video (`VNxBZ7ka5J0`)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VNxBZ7ka5J0) & [YC Startup Library](https://www.google.com/search?q=https://www.ycombinator.com/library/VNxBZ7ka5J0)
* **Channel:** Y Combinator / Startup School

#### 1. Quantifying "Make Something People Want"
David opens with the core dilemma of early-stage startups:
> *"YC has a famously simple motto: **Make something people want**. It's the purest statement of the job of startup founders. But what gets talked about a lot less is: **how do you know if you've actually done it?** The single best way that I have found to answer this question quantitatively is **cohort retention**... In my own startup, I didn't understand it until many years later when it was nearly too late. I remember pitching a prestigious VC firm for our Series A, and they asked me: 'Hey Dave, how's your cohort retention?' I gave a completely hand-wavy answer, went back to my laptop, Googled 'cohort retention', and realized that what I said must have made zero sense. I hope to save you that mistake."* ([YouTube `VNxBZ7ka5J0` at 00:09 & 01:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VNxBZ7ka5J0))

#### 2. The Three Parameters of Rigorous Cohort Measurement
To measure retention without deceiving yourself, define three variables:
1. **Cohort Isolation:** Grouping users by the exact week or month they first joined (and later slicing by country, device, or acquisition channel).
2. **The Value-Correlated Action:** Not merely "opening the app" or visiting a landing page.
   > *"Pick an action that is deeply correlated with the user actually receiving value:
   > * **Instagram:** Viewed 3 or more posts (filtering out people who opened the app and left immediately).
   > * **Uber:** Completed a ride.
   > * **Google Photos:** Tapped in and viewed a photo full-screen.
   > If a user just launches your app and bounces, they are not an active user. Count only actions where real value was delivered."* ([YouTube `VNxBZ7ka5J0` at 04:33 & 05:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VNxBZ7ka5J0))
3. **The Natural Usage Frequency (Time Granularity):** Match the natural cadence of the use case:
   * **Daily:** Social media and messaging (TikTok, Instagram, Slack).
   * **Weekly:** Utilities and productivity (Google Photos, Uber, project management).
   * **Quarterly / Annually:** Travel and episodic transactions (Airbnb, TurboTax).

#### 3. The Shape of the Curve is the Only Thing That Matters
David delivers his most famous analytical doctrine:
> *"The only thing that matters — and I can't stress this enough — is **whether your cohort curves get flat**. The shape of the curve is what matters, not the absolute height.
> Consider two products:
> * **Product A:** Retains 85% in month 1, 70% in month 2, 55% in month 3, and continues sloping downward. It looks great early on, but **because the line is sloped downward, it will eventually hit zero**. Every single user will churn. That company is doomed.
> * **Product B:** Drops off a cliff immediately to 20% in month 1, but then stays flat at 20% in month 2, month 3, month 6, and month 12.
> Product B is the winner! A flat curve means you are accumulating a permanent, compounding user base. Product A is just running on a customer acquisition treadmill that will eventually run out of cash."* ([YouTube `VNxBZ7ka5J0` at 10:43 & 11:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VNxBZ7ka5J0))

#### 4. The Google Photos 20% Epiphany
> *"In the early days of Google Photos, our weekly cohort curves dropped sharply and flattened out between 20% and 40%. If you tell a friend, '80% of my users leave immediately,' it sounds terrible. But the fact that 20% of users stayed and used Google Photos every single week indefinitely gave me absolute confidence: **we had a product that 20% of all humans on Earth would use forever**. Four years later, we crossed a billion users. Getting your curve flat gives you the mathematical right to scale."* ([YouTube `VNxBZ7ka5J0` at 13:01 & 13:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VNxBZ7ka5J0))

#### 5. The Four Ways Founders Delude Themselves
David warns founders against vanity rationalizations:
1. **Widening the Cohort Time Window:**
   > *"At Bump, our weekly cohorts looked bad. So we looked at monthly cohorts, and they looked a bit better. Then we said, 'Well, maybe quarterly usage is reasonable,' and the curves looked great! But we were lying to ourselves. Bump was a utility meant to be used frequently. Expanding the time window is just hiding churn from yourself."* ([YouTube `VNxBZ7ka5J0` at 15:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VNxBZ7ka5J0))
2. **Counting Trivial Push/Notification Clicks:**
   > *"At Google, there was a point where active Google+ users were counted based on whether someone clicked the little red notification bell in Gmail. It made executive dashboards look massive, but it was a complete lie. Those were not active social network users."* ([YouTube `VNxBZ7ka5J0` at 16:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VNxBZ7ka5J0))
3. **Assuming 'Is Paying' Equals Active Usage:**
   > *"Users stop using a product long before they stop paying for it (think of unused gym memberships or forgotten Netflix subscriptions). Don't track 'is paying' alone; track 'is paying AND actively engaged in core workflows'."* ([YouTube `VNxBZ7ka5J0` at 17:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VNxBZ7ka5J0))
4. **Blindly Trusting Analytics Dashboards:**
   > *"Build your first cohort triangle charts manually in Google Sheets or Python SQL logs. You won't understand what rolling retention or date filters are actually masking until you build the raw matrix yourself."* ([YouTube `VNxBZ7ka5J0` at 20:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VNxBZ7ka5J0))

#### 6. The User Persona Mismatch (The Gen Z Trap)
> *"Often your product is great, but your acquisition channel is dragging down your cohorts. At Google Photos, a senior marketing executive decided we needed to target Gen Z. We spent heavily and brought in millions of young users, but their cohort retention was abysmal. Why? Because teenagers don't have 20 years of life memories to reminisce about! When we shifted marketing back to parents and adults with families, our retention curves immediately surged. **Fixing customer acquisition targeting is often the fastest way to flatten a retention curve.**"* ([YouTube `VNxBZ7ka5J0` at 22:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VNxBZ7ka5J0))

### 1. What Makes David Lieb Say "Yes"
1. **Unflinching Honesty About Cohort Retention:** If you present a cohort retention chart, admit that your month-1 retention drops to 25%, but demonstrate that months 2 through 6 remain completely flat at 25%, David will immediately respect you. He knows flat curves build billion-user companies.
2. **A Crisp, Value-Correlated Action:** Explaining that you do *not* count vanity pageviews or app launches, but specifically track when a user completes the core magical action of your product.
3. **Radical Simplicity in User Experience:** David built Google Photos to be so intuitive that a grandparent could back up and search 10,000 photos with zero instructions. If your consumer product requires a 5-step tutorial, you are losing.
4. **Acquisition-Persona Alignment:** Demonstrating that you know exactly *who* retains best on your product, and that you have focused your marketing on that high-retention persona rather than spraying ads at the wrong demographic.
5. **Deep Technical Taste in Computer Vision & Agents:** Given his Stanford AI Lab background, David loves technical teams solving hard perceptual, mobile OS, or inference challenges.

### 2. Immediate Disqualifiers ("Red Flags")
* **Bragging About Vanity Top-of-Funnel Numbers:** Boasting about 100,000 registered users, 50,000 app downloads, or viral TikTok views without knowing your 30-day cohort retention. (David: *"Bump had 150 million downloads and was still failing"*).
* **Down-Sloping Cohort Curves:** Presenting curves that retain 60% in week 1, 40% in week 2, 20% in week 3, and 5% in week 4 without flattening. That is a dying business.
* **Gaming Metrics with Push Notifications:** Driving users to open the app via clickbait notifications or badges, counting that as "active engagement."
* **Over-Complicated First-Mile Onboarding:** Requiring users to configure complex settings, invite friends, or fill out long surveys before they experience the product's core value.

## 6. Question Bank & 15-Second Answer Templates

Master these rapid-fire answers tailored specifically for David Lieb's interview room:

### Question 1: *"How do you know you've made something people want?"*
* **The Goal:** 15 seconds. Quote flat cohort retention and the value-correlated action.
* **Weak Answer:** *"We have over 20,000 signups, our growth is 15% week-over-week, and users leave great reviews in the App Store."*
* **Optimal Answer (Tailored for David):**
  > *"Because our 60-day cohort retention curve flattens at 28%. We define an active user not by app opens, but by whether they log a completed workout and receive real-time AI feedback. That 28% cohort has used our tool every week for four months."*

### Question 2: *"What is the core action that defines an active user in your product?"*
* **The Goal:** Prove you filter out shallow engagement.
* **Optimal Answer (Tailored for David):**
  > *"Viewing a generated summary does not count. A user is only counted as active if they click 'Accept' on an automated legal case summary and export it into their court filing ledger. That proves our output was accurate enough to enter their live legal workflow."*

### Question 3: *"Bump had 150 million downloads and struggled with retention. Why won't your consumer app suffer the same fate?"*
* **The Goal:** Explain the shift from novelty utility to daily habit.
* **Optimal Answer (Tailored for David):**
  > *"Bump required a physical co-located partner and an episodic transaction. Our app operates ambiently over iMessage, sending proactive daily workout prompts based on Apple Health activity. We create daily value single-player, driving a steady daily habit rather than an episodic novelty."*

### Question 4: *"Why do 70% of your users drop off in the first week?"*
* **The Goal:** Demonstrate persona alignment and acquisition channel awareness.
* **Optimal Answer (Tailored for David):**
  > *"Our early drop-off was driven by generic Reddit traffic that didn't have gym memberships. When we isolate users acquired through powerlifting and marathon communities, 30-day retention jumps from 18% to 42%. We've shut down broad social ads to focus exclusively on high-intent fitness communities."*

### Question 5: *"Why conversational AI over iMessage rather than a standalone mobile app?"*
* **The Goal:** Address post-app mobile fatigue.
* **Optimal Answer (Tailored for David):**
  > *"Because users have app fatigue. The average user downloads zero new apps per month, and notification push permissions have 80% drop-off. iMessage has 98% open rates with zero installation friction, allowing our AI coach to integrate directly into their daily conversation stream."*

## 7. Strategic Action Checklist Before You Face David Lieb

- [ ] **Plot Your Cohort Retention Curves:** Build a real cohort triangle chart. Ensure you know the exact percentage where your curves flatten out over 30, 60, and 90 days.
- [ ] **Define Your Value-Correlated Action:** Ensure your active user metric measures real customer utility, not superficial visits, app launches, or notification clicks.
- [ ] **Eliminate First-Mile Onboarding Friction:** Audit your user onboarding flow. Can a first-time user reach the core "aha moment" in under 30 seconds with zero instructions?
- [ ] **Slice Cohorts by Acquisition Channel:** Know which customer segment retains best, and be prepared to explain why you are doubling down on that specific persona.
- [ ] **Embrace Brutal Metric Honesty:** Speak with humility about your churn. Never attempt to disguise downward-sloping retention curves with vanity cumulative user numbers.
